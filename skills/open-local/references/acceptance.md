# What belongs in the registry

A source is accepted when all of these hold.

1. **It is public.** Anyone can read it on the open web without an account. Paywalled
   newsrooms are welcome (record `paywall`); login-only groups are not.
2. **It is about a place.** It regularly carries information about the place or area it is
   listed for. A national outlet that mentioned the town once does not qualify.
3. **It is identifiable.** There is a named publisher: a public body, an organisation, a
   business, a newsroom, or a named individual publishing in public as a journalist or
   writer.
4. **It is honest about what it is.** `classification` says whether it is first-hand
   (`primary`), a listing or rewrite of others (`secondary`) or both (`mixed`). Sites that
   are mostly scraped, machine-generated or copied content are not accepted. Sites that
   exist to impersonate a newsroom or a public body are not accepted.
5. **It works.** The home page and every listed endpoint loaded on the `verified` date.
6. **It respects people.** No sources whose main content is private individuals: personal
   social accounts, mugshot sites, neighbour-dispute forums, people-search pages.
7. **It can be listed.** The registry stores only facts about the source (its name, where
   it is, what it covers). If an owner asks for their source to be removed, it is retired.

## Not a judgement of quality or viewpoint

Listing is not endorsement. The registry does not rank sources by politics or by how much
anyone likes them. A partisan local blog that is public, identifiable and about the place
can be listed, typed accurately (`blog`) and classified accurately. Readers and agents
decide how to weigh it; the rules in this skill tell agents to label opinion
as opinion.

## Disputes

If people disagree about whether a source belongs, or how it is described, open an issue.
Maintainers decide using this page, and the decision and reasons are recorded in the
issue. See GOVERNANCE.md.

## Removing and retiring

Sources that disappear are marked `retired`, not deleted, so their ids are never reused
and history stays readable. Sources that are merely broken are marked `broken` until
someone fixes the address.
