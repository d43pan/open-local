# Roadmap

A direction, not a promise. Open an issue to argue with any of it.

## Now (0.1)

- One skill, `open-local`, that does three jobs: answer questions about a place, map a new
  place, and grow coverage to the next towns over.
- Registry format, validation, exports (JSON, OPML, CSV, GeoJSON).
- Tools: feed discovery, recent items, dates, page changes, availability, proposals.
- First mapped community: Little Compton, Rhode Island, with Tiverton, Westport (MA) and
  the regional sources around them.

## Next

- **Work the frontier.** Fall River and Dartmouth, MA and Bristol and Middletown, RI are
  recorded as the next places out. Portsmouth, RI was the first town added this way.
- **Ten communities in five places that are organised differently**, at least three
  outside the US, to test that the place model holds (no counties, parishes, two
  languages, a city neighbourhood).
- **First community maintainers.** Invite a librarian, a local reporter and a town clerk
  to look after their places; add `CODEOWNERS`; write down what they found awkward.
- **Meeting records.** Many councils publish only PDFs on a portal. Add endpoint notes
  and helpers for the common platforms, and link to existing open projects for
  transcripts instead of rebuilding them.
- **Interoperate:** import and export against Open Civic Data division ids and Wikidata;
  offer the place-to-sources lookup in a form other civic projects can consume.
- **Better evaluation:** a larger set of graded questions, run against more than one agent.

## Later

- Boundaries for places that publish them under a compatible licence, so "near me" stops
  relying on centre points.
- Per-source notes on how to read a site that has no feed.
- Languages other than English for the skill and guides.
- A neutral home: a steering group from community maintainers and the repository under an
  organisation, once there are enough maintainers for that to mean something.

## Not planned

- A hosted API, an account system, or anything that requires this project's servers.
- Storing or republishing articles.
- Ranking sources by quality or viewpoint.
- Generating news.
