# Adding your town: a guide for everyone

You know your town. That is the hard part. This guide is for residents, librarians,
journalists, clerks and volunteers who have never used GitHub.

## What you are adding

A list of the public places where local information appears, each with its web address:

- the town or city website, and where meeting agendas and minutes are posted
- the newspaper or news site people actually read
- the library, the school district, the recreation department
- community groups, the historical society, land trusts
- farm stands, markets, restaurants and cafes that post their own hours and events
- galleries, theatres, music series, festivals
- anything else a neighbour would tell a newcomer to check

You are not writing articles and you are not vouching for anyone. You are saying "this is
where it gets posted".

## The easiest way: a form

1. Make a free account at github.com (an email address is all it needs).
2. Go to https://github.com/d43pan/open-local/issues/new/choose
3. Choose **Add a community** (for a whole town) or **Add a source** (for one site).
4. Fill in the boxes and press **Submit**.

A maintainer turns it into registry entries and may ask a question or two. You will get
an email when it is in.

## With an AI assistant

If you use an AI agent that supports skills (Claude, Codex and others), install Open Local
(see the README) and say:

> Use open-local-build to add my town to Open Local.

It will ask you where you look for local information, go and check each site, find the
feeds and calendars, and show you a list to correct. When you are happy it sends the
proposal for review. You stay in charge: nothing is sent without your say, and a person
reviews it before it is accepted.

## What makes a good source

- **Public:** anyone can read it without logging in. (A newspaper with a paywall is fine.
  A private Facebook group is not.)
- **About your place:** it regularly has something about your town or area.
- **First-hand where possible:** the board's own minutes page beats a site that copies it.
- **Not about private people:** no personal accounts, no neighbour-dispute groups.

## Looking after a place

If you would like to be the person who keeps your town's list accurate, say so in the
form. Community maintainers review changes for places they know. It takes a few minutes
when something changes, and the web page is all you need. See
[GOVERNANCE.md](../GOVERNANCE.md).

## Questions people ask

**Will this take readers away from our paper?** The skills are built to send people to
the original: short summaries in the agent's own words, a link to the article, the
newsroom named as the source, paywalls respected.

**Can I remove my organisation's site?** Yes. Open a "Broken or wrong source" issue and
choose "The owner asked for it to be removed".

**Does being listed mean being endorsed?** No. The list says where information appears,
not whether you should agree with it.

**What does it cost?** Nothing. The data is in the public domain and the code is open
source.
